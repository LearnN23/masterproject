SECTION 1: SUMMARY OF EMPIRICAL JOB ADVERTISEMENT FINDINGS (\\(N = 29\\))
1.1 Empirical Corpus Overview
The instructional design of this EOP learning module is strictly grounded in the empirical findings derived from \\(N = 29\\) unique included job advertisements for Thai-speaking Customer Support (CS), Order-to-Cash (OTC), and IT Service Desk roles based in Malaysia. All analyzed roles require daily oral and written English communication within multinational Business Process Outsourcing (BPO) centers, Shared Service Centres (SSCs), or IT helpdesks operating in Kuala Lumpur, Selangor, and Cyberjaya.
1.2 Summary of Occupational Communication Tasks (\\(N = 29\\))
Content analysis of the \\(N = 29\\) included job postings revealed 10 primary occupational communication tasks. Tasks 1 through 9 exhibit a 100.0% prevalence rate (\\(29/29\\) ads), establishing them as non-negotiable core competencies for Thai IT graduates seeking employment in Malaysia. Task 10 (Troubleshooting technical issues) exhibits an 86.2% prevalence rate (\\(25/29\\) ads), appearing universally across IT Service Desk positions (\\(10/10\\) ads) and e-Commerce/SaaS support roles (\\(15/19\\) ads).

1.3 Communicative Language Functions (Explicit vs. Inferred)
Language functions were coded based on explicit statements in job duty descriptions or analytical inferences derived from required workplace tasks:
Explicitly Stated Functions: Summarizing and documenting information (\\(100\%\\)), Apologizing and responding to complaints (\\(93.1\%\\)), Escalating cases (\\(93.1\%\\)), Suggesting solutions (\\(89.7\%\\)), Describing symptoms or errors (\\(86.2\%\\)).
Analytically Inferred Micro-Functions (\\(100\%\\) across all roles): Asking for account information, Asking clarifying questions, Confirming understanding, Requesting repetition, Explaining technical root causes, Giving step-by-step instructions, Checking whether a fix works, Reassuring customers, Requesting internal assistance, and Closing an interaction politely.
1.4 Macro Skills and Linguistic Resources
Four Macro Skills (\\(100\%\\) Demand): Listening (aural comprehension of diverse customer accents over VoIP/phone), Reading (visual comprehension of emails, tickets, order forms, SOPs), Writing (graphic production of ticket logs, emails, live chats), and Speaking (oral production for phone calls, internal escalations, and oral case handovers).
Target Linguistic Resources:
General Workplace &amp; CS Acronyms: SLA (Service Level Agreement), KPI, FCR (First Contact Resolution), AHT (Average Handling Time), TAT (Turnaround Time), CSAT (Customer Satisfaction).
Domain Technical Jargon: Active Directory, IP Configuration, VPN Gateway, SAP S/4HANA, Salesforce, Zendesk, Waybill, Reconciliation, Prescription parameters.
Professional Registers: Empathetic de-escalation register for complaints; directive imperative register for troubleshooting guidance; concise past-tense reporting register for CRM documentation.

Measurable Behavioral Learning Objectives by Lesson
Lesson 1 (120 Mins): Professional Customer Enquiries &amp; Call Openings
LO1.1: Identify standard brand greetings and account verification protocols in contact center interactions.
LO1.2: Formulate polite opening statements and identity verification requests using modal verbs (may, could).
LO1.3: Perform a 30-second professional call opening and account verification in a simulated customer call.
Lesson 2 (150 Mins): Identifying &amp; Clarifying Technical Problems
LO2.1: Differentiate between open-ended diagnostic questions and closed confirmation questions.
LO2.2: Construct indirect questions (Could you tell me...) to elicit error symptoms without sounding interrogation-like.
LO2.3: Perform a 45-second diagnostic clarification response to isolate a user authentication fault.
Lesson 3 (150 Mins): Troubleshooting &amp; Explaining Technical Problems
LO3.1: Analyze customer error descriptions and classify them into hardware, software, or network categories.
LO3.2: Explain technical root causes in plain English using conditional structures (If you reset..., the gateway will...).
LO3.3: Perform a 60-second troubleshooting response proposing a temporary workaround for an application crash.
Lesson 4 (120 Mins): Handling Complaints &amp; Responding Empathetically
LO4.1: Identify empathetic de-escalation expressions versus defensive/unhelpful customer responses.
LO4.2: Formulate formal apologies and reassurance statements (I understand your frustration; rest assured...).
LO4.3: Perform a 60-second de-escalation response to a customer experiencing a critical shipment delay.
Lesson 5 (120 Mins): Giving Clear Instructions &amp; Checking Understanding
LO5.1: Apply imperative verbs and sequential transition markers (First, Next, Subsequently, Finally).
LO5.2: Construct comprehension-check questions (Does that option appear on your screen?).
LO5.3: Perform a 60-second instructional spoken response guiding a user through a multi-factor authentication setup.
Lesson 6 (120 Mins): Escalating Unresolved Cases &amp; Explaining Next Steps
LO6.1: Explain SLA escalation triggers and Tier-2 transfer protocols clearly to end-users.
LO6.2: Formulate professional transfer explanations managing customer expectations without assigning internal blame.
LO6.3: Perform a 60-second escalation speech informing a client that their case is being transferred to Network Operations.
Lesson 7 (120 Mins): Occupational Vocabulary, Case Records &amp; Ticket Hygiene
LO7.1: Define and apply key contact center acronyms (SLA, FCR, AHT, TAT, CSAT) in workplace contexts.
LO7.2: Document structured CRM ticket notes using past-tense diagnostic verbs and professional formatting.
LO7.3: Produce a complete, grammatically accurate written CRM ticket log from raw customer call transcripts.
Lesson 8 (240 Mins): Integrated Support Process, Ticket Logs &amp; Oral Case Summaries
LO8.1: Synthesize complex multi-turn customer interaction logs and CRM records into essential problem/resolution points.
LO8.2: Structure an oral case summary following the 5-stage framework (Problem \\(\rightarrow\\) Symptoms \\(\rightarrow\\) Actions Taken \\(\rightarrow\\) Current Status \\(\rightarrow\\) Next Step).
LO8.3: Perform a 90-second professional oral case summary based on a simulated CRM ticket record for a shift handover.

Policy on Thai Accent: In accordance with International EOP standards, a Thai accent is not penalized if the speech remains intelligible and clear. Assessment focuses on pragmatic effectiveness and communication success rather than native-speaker accent imitation.
5.2 Critical Review of Rubric
Strengths: Explicitly measures workplace communicative competence rather than abstract grammar alone; distinguishes pragmatic tone (critical in CS) from technical accuracy.
Limitations: Requires raters to balance multiple dimensions during a 60–90 second recording; requires pre-rating calibration to maintain inter-rater reliability.
Evaluability from Audio: All 5 criteria are directly observable from recorded audio files.
Construct Overlap: Minimal. Criterion 1 evaluates Content, Criterion 2 evaluates Pragmatic Tone, Criterion 3 evaluates Lexicon, Criterion 4 evaluates Syntax, and Criterion 5 evaluates Aural Clarity/Flow.
Validation Disclaimer: This rubric represents a researcher-designed instrument structured for expert content validation and pilot testing. Empirical reliability coefficients (e.g., Cronbach's \\(\alpha\\), Inter-Rater ICC) will be computed following data collection.
5.3 Scoring, Aggregation, and Gain Score Framework
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                 SCORING AGGREGATION MATHEMATICS                                   |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  1. Single Scenario Maximum Score:    5 Criteria x 5 Points = 25 Points                           |
|  2. Full Test Maximum Raw Score:      8 Scenarios x 25 Points = 200 Raw Points                   |
|  3. Mean Scenario Score Formula:      Mean Score = (Sum of 8 Scenario Scores) / 8  [Range: 5-25]  |
|  4. Percentage Score Formula:         Percentage (%) = (Raw Total Score / 200) x 100             |
|  5. Pre-to-Post Gain Score Formula:   Delta Score = Post-Test Score - Pre-Test Score             |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
Protocol for Missing or Unusable Recordings
Non-submission / Silence: Scored as \\(1\\) point per criterion (minimum raw score = \\(5\\) per scenario). A score of \\(0\\) is not assigned because the analytic scale operates on a \\(1-5\\) range.
Unusable Audio (Technical Glitch): The student is prompted to re-record the response under identical standardized preparation conditions.
Dual-Rater Inter-Rater Reliability Protocol
Two independent raters score each recording using the rubric.
Individual ratings are preserved for Inter-Rater Reliability analysis using Intraclass Correlation Coefficients (ICC Two-Way Random, Absolute Agreement) or Cohen's Weighted Kappa (\\(\kappa_w\\)).
Final reported student score = Average of Rater 1 and Rater 2 scores:\\\text{Final Scenario Score} = \frac{\text{Rater 1 Score} + \text{Rater 2 Score}}{2}\\
